# Finance Projects | Jake Hiu

I use financial analysis to understand how a business makes money, where its risks sit, and whether its assumptions hold up. I care about being able to explain the numbers and connect them to a business decision.

This portfolio brings together two academic team projects: an Excel framework for evaluating a Costco warehouse expansion and a financial analysis report covering Funko, Boeing, and FedEx. Each project folder includes the original deliverable and a guide to the work.

## Projects

| Project | Business question | Methods | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Costco Warehouse Expansion](./costco-warehouse-expansion/) | How should a proposed 10-warehouse expansion be evaluated? | Assumption development, CAPM, WACC, incremental cash flow planning | Excel workbook |
| [UW Financial Analysis](./uw-financial-analysis/) | What do financial statements, competitors, and management commentary reveal about business performance? | Financial ratios, horizontal analysis, peer benchmarking, earnings-call review | PDF report |

## My contributions

**Costco:** I developed the project assumptions by comparing the available company data with the proposed warehouse expansion. I used that evidence to explain my choices and recommend a base case for evaluating the investment.

**UW Finance:** I completed the Funko analysis and contributed to the report summaries. My work connected profitability, liquidity, debt, and cash flow with the questions I thought management needed to answer.

## How I approach the work

- Start with the business question and identify the numbers that matter.
- Explain the assumptions behind a calculation.
- Compare profitability with cash flow, liquidity, and financing risk.
- Question management's explanation when the financial evidence leaves something unresolved.
- Make the analysis understandable to someone reviewing the project for the first time.

## Project context

Both projects were completed in teams. The original files retain their team credits. The project guides describe the submitted work without claiming that I individually authored every section.

The Costco workbook is a preliminary investment-analysis framework. It contains a WACC calculation and documented assumptions, but does not yet contain a completed discounted cash flow valuation, NPV, or IRR. The UW report is the original academic submission. Its project guide identifies internal inconsistencies that readers should consider when reviewing the results.
